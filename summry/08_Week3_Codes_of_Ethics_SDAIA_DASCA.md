## 1. Why Codes of Ethics Matter for Data Professionals

> From Week 2: A code of ethics takes ethics that are "governed by a particular and definitive regiment of rules."

### The Gap They Fill

| Without a Code | With a Code |
|---------------|------------|
| Professionals improvise ethical decisions | Consistent, principled decision-making |
| No accountability standard | Clear expectations for behavior |
| Clients have no assurance | Trust is built through visible commitment |
| New practitioners have no guidance | Educational baseline exists |

### How Codes Relate to Ethical Theories (Week 2 Connection)
Codes of ethics are **built on** the ethical theories studied in Week 2:
- DASCA's "do no harm" principle ← **Consequentialism** (avoid harmful outcomes)
- "Act with integrity" ← **Deontology** (duty-based obligations)
- "Respect cultural diversity" ← **Relativism** (norms vary by context)
- "Promote human wellbeing" ← **Utilitarianism** (maximize benefit to all)

## 2. DASCA Code of Ethics

> **DASCA** = Data Science Council of America  
> A global credentialing and standards body for data science professionals

### Why DASCA Matters
- One of the few professional bodies that has formalized a **Code of Ethics specifically for data scientists**
- Comparable to how ACM has a code for computer scientists, or AMA for physicians
- Establishes that **data science is a profession** with ethical obligations, not just a technical skill

### Core Principles of the DASCA Code of Ethics

#### Principle 1: Act with Integrity
- Be honest and transparent in all professional activities
- Do not misrepresent your qualifications, methods, or findings
- **Connection to Week 2:** Deontology — integrity is a duty regardless of outcome
- **Why it matters in data:** Overstating model accuracy or hiding biases can cause real harm

#### Principle 2: Do No Harm
- Consider the potential negative impacts of data science work on individuals, communities, and society
- Apply precautionary thinking when consequences are uncertain
- **Connection to Week 2:** Consequentialism — judge actions by their outcomes
- **Why it matters:** Biased hiring algorithms, discriminatory credit scoring, surveillance tools

#### Principle 3: Protect Privacy and Confidentiality
- Respect individuals' right to privacy in data collection, storage, and use
- Handle confidential information responsibly
- **Connection to Week 4:** Privacy = Solitude + Anonymity + Intimacy + Reserve
- **Saudi connection:** Aligns with SDAIA's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)

#### Principle 4: Promote Fairness and Avoid Bias
- Be vigilant about bias in data, models, and conclusions
- Strive for equitable outcomes across all groups
- **Connection to Week 5:** Seven Sources of Harm in ML — all lead to unfairness

#### Principle 5: Maintain Transparency and Accountability
- Be clear about data sources, methods, limitations, and assumptions
- Accept responsibility for the work you produce
- **Connection to Week 14:** Problem of Many Hands — transparency counters this
- **Connection to Week 5:** Datasheets for Datasets = operationalizing transparency

#### Principle 6: Respect Intellectual Property
- Credit original sources and respect ownership rights
- Do not misuse others' data, code, or models
- **Connection to Week 9:** IP law — copyright, trade secrets, patents

#### Principle 7: Advance the Profession
- Contribute to the development of the data science community
- Share knowledge and best practices
- Support training and education

#### Principle 8: Serve the Public Good
- Go beyond client interests to consider broader societal impact
- **Connection to Week 16:** Cybersecurity professionals' obligations to the public
- **Connection to Stakeholder Analysis:** Porter's Shared Value — serve society, not just shareholders

### DASCA vs. ACM Code of Ethics

| | DASCA | ACM |
|--|-------|-----|
| **Scope** | Data science professionals | Computer science broadly |
| **Founded** | Data-specific focus | 1947, updated 1992 |
| **Imperatives** | 8 core principles | 24 imperatives |
| **Focus** | Data, privacy, bias, fairness | Computing broadly + society |
| **Saudi relevance** | Referenced in course | Referenced in Week 2 slides |

## 3. SDAIA AI Principles (2025)

> **SDAIA** = Saudi Data and AI Authority  
> Saudi Arabia's national authority governing data and AI policy  
> These principles establish the **national ethical framework for AI** in the Kingdom

### Context: Why Saudi Arabia Needs Its Own AI Principles

- Saudi Arabia is investing heavily in AI through **Vision 2030**
- Need to balance **technological advancement** with **Islamic values, cultural norms, and legal obligations**
- SDAIA is the regulatory body responsible for ensuring AI development aligns with national values
- The PDPL (Personal Data Protection Law) is SDAIA's regulatory instrument for data

### SDAIA's Core AI Ethics Principles (2025)

#### Principle 1: Human-Centricity (المحورية الإنسانية)
- AI must serve human needs and enhance human wellbeing
- Humans remain in control of AI systems — not the reverse
- **Connection:** Natural Law (Week 2) — human dignity is non-negotiable
- **Practical meaning:** AI in healthcare, finance, and government must be designed with human oversight

#### Principle 2: Fairness and Non-Discrimination (العدالة وعدم التمييز)
- AI systems must treat all individuals and groups equitably
- Must not perpetuate or amplify social, gender, or racial biases
- **Connection:** Week 5 — Seven Sources of Harm, especially Historical Bias and Evaluation Bias
- **Saudi context:** Applies across all demographic groups in KSA

#### Principle 3: Transparency and Explainability (الشفافية وقابلية التفسير)
- AI decisions must be explainable to those affected
- Organizations must be transparent about when and how AI is used
- **Connection:** Week 14 — Black Box problem in Deep Learning
- **Practical meaning:** A citizen denied a government service by AI has the right to understand why

#### Principle 4: Privacy and Data Protection (الخصوصية وحماية البيانات)
- AI systems must comply with PDPL
- Personal data used for AI must be collected with proper consent
- **Connection:** Week 4 — Privacy types; GDPR/PDPL requirements
- **Saudi instrument:** The Implementing Regulation of the PDPL

#### Principle 5: Safety and Reliability (السلامة والموثوقية)
- AI systems must be safe, robust, and reliable before deployment
- Must be tested rigorously — especially in high-stakes domains (health, transport, finance)
- **Connection:** Week 5 — Deployment Bias (using a model beyond its tested scope)

#### Principle 6: Accountability and Responsibility (المساءلة والمسؤولية)
- Clear chains of responsibility for AI decisions must exist
- Organizations deploying AI are accountable for its outcomes
- **Connection:** Week 14 — Problem of Many Hands; Week 16 — Accountability in cybersecurity

#### Principle 7: Sustainability and Societal Benefit (الاستدامة والفائدة الاجتماعية)
- AI development must consider long-term societal and environmental impacts
- Must align with Vision 2030's sustainable development goals
- **Connection:** Stakeholder Analysis — Porter's Shared Value; serve society, not just profit

### SDAIA vs. GDPR

| | SDAIA / PDPL | GDPR |
|--|-------------|------|
| **Jurisdiction** | Saudi Arabia | European Union |
| **Focus** | Data protection + AI ethics | Data protection |
| **Cultural context** | Islamic values, Vision 2030 | Secular European values |
| **AI-specific?** | Yes — SDAIA AI Principles separate document | No — AI Act separate |
| **Consent model** | Explicit consent required | Explicit consent required |
| **Right to explanation** | Yes (AI decisions) | Yes (automated decisions, GDPR Art. 22) |

### SDAIA Personal Data Breach Incidents
- SDAIA publishes annual reports on data breach cases in Saudi Arabia
- Organizations that suffer breaches must notify SDAIA and affected individuals
- **2024 Disclosure Cases:** Used as real-world examples of data ethics failures in the Saudi context

## 4. DASCA + SDAIA Applied Together

### Scenario: A Saudi company uses AI to screen job applications

| Question | DASCA Response | SDAIA/PDPL Response |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|
| Is the AI model biased? | Principle 4: Must promote fairness and avoid bias | Fairness principle — must not discriminate |
| Are applicants' data protected? | Principle 3: Privacy and confidentiality | PDPL: Consent required; data minimization |
| Can applicants understand why they were rejected? | Principle 5: Transparency | Transparency principle: AI decisions must be explainable |
| Who is responsible if harm occurs? | Principle 5: Accountability | Accountability principle + PDPL enforcement by SDAIA |
| Does the AI serve Saudi society? | Principle 8: Public good | Societal benefit principle aligned with Vision 2030 |
